My current research develops around the relationship between artificial intelligence, institutions, and social transformation. Rather than treating AI only as a technical tool, I am interested in how emerging technologies reshape governance capacity, educational evaluation, labour relations, public decision-making, and the organization of social life.

The archive currently follows three connected lines of inquiry.

First, I examine AI and governance. My recent questions focus on whether AI can improve public decision-making, whether it strengthens state capacity, and whether the central challenge of technology governance lies in scientific uncertainty or institutional design. These questions connect computer science with public policy, comparative governance, and science and technology studies.

Second, I study education and human judgment in the age of AI. My essay *When Answers Are No Longer Scarce* argues that generative AI weakens the scarcity of final answers while increasing the importance of process, judgment, context, and responsibility. This line of inquiry asks what education should evaluate when AI can assist with writing, coding, research, and problem solving.

Third, I explore political economy and human development. My reflection on *The Communist Manifesto* connects Marxist political economy with contemporary questions about capital, labour, education, and AI. The central question is whether human value can transcend capital logic, and whether new technologies can help human beings become more fully human rather than more machine-like.

Recent academic training, including summer-school study in technology governance and policy-related topics, has further pushed my work toward comparative governance, expert authority, technology assessment, and institutional design. I am increasingly interested in why different countries respond differently to the same scientific evidence, how experts should participate in public decision-making, and how institutions can govern uncertain emerging technologies.

At this stage, my work remains exploratory. The goal of this website is not to present finished conclusions, but to document the formation of research questions, reading trajectories, working hypotheses, and early analytical frameworks. Over time, I hope this archive will show how my research develops from questions into more systematic academic work.
