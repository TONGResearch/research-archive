question_description: >-
  Emerging technologies are often too complex for ordinary citizens or policymakers
  to fully understand without expert input. Yet technologies also produce social,
  ethical, political, and economic consequences that cannot be governed by expertise alone.
  This question investigates how much authority experts should have in technology governance.

scope: >-
  This research focuses on expert authority, democratic legitimacy, public participation,
  accountability, technocracy, and the relationship between specialized knowledge and
  political decision-making.

objectives: >-
  To examine how technology governance can use expert knowledge without allowing expert
  authority to replace democratic judgment, public accountability, or political legitimacy.

current_position: >-
  My current view is that experts should have strong epistemic authority but limited political
  authority. Experts can clarify evidence, uncertainty, risk, and technical feasibility, but
  the final goals and trade-offs of governance require broader democratic and institutional judgment.
