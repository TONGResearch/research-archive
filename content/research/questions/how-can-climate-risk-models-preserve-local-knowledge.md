question_description: >-
  Climate risk models require defined variables, spatial units, and comparable data.
  Local accounts may instead describe a particular entrance that floods, a route that
  becomes inaccessible, or a household whose needs are absent from official records.
  This question asks what happens when such knowledge is converted into model inputs:
  what can be represented, what becomes distorted, and what should remain available
  alongside the model rather than being forced into a numerical field? It connects my
  interest in local climate adaptation with the problem of representing social and
  physical environments in computational systems.
scope: >-
  The initial setting is neighbourhood-level flood and heat adaptation. The scope includes
  spatial scale, data schemas, missing observations, the provenance of reports, differences
  between accounts, and opportunities for residents to correct records. Local knowledge
  will not be assumed to be uniform or automatically accurate; equally, absence from a
  dataset will not be treated as evidence that a need or risk does not exist.
objectives: >-
  To compare ways of combining sensor and administrative data with resident reports,
  participatory mapping, and qualitative accounts; identify information lost through
  aggregation or classification; and outline a data workflow that preserves sources,
  uncertainty, and opportunities for correction. A possible later exercise is to compare
  alternative representations of the same hypothetical neighbourhood and examine how
  they alter adaptation priorities.
current_position: >-
  My provisional position is that incorporating local knowledge requires more than adding
  another input variable. A system should preserve context, make disagreement visible,
  and allow people to challenge its representation of their circumstances. Some knowledge
  may be better retained as linked qualitative evidence or a reason for human review than
  converted into a single score. I want to investigate where this approach is useful and
  what practical limits it faces.
