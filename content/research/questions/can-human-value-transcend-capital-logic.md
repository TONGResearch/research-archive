question_description: |-
  Modern societies increasingly evaluate individuals, professions, and institutions through the logic of markets, productivity, and capital.
  Yet should human worth ultimately be measured by economic value?
  Are there forms of dignity, creativity, responsibility, and freedom that exist beyond commodification?
  This research explores whether human value can transcend capital logic and what this question means in the age of artificial intelligence.
scope: |-
  This research examines political economy, political philosophy, labor, education, artificial intelligence, public policy, and digital capitalism.
  Rather than rejecting markets, it investigates whether capital should remain the dominant criterion for evaluating human beings.
objectives: |-
  To examine how capital shapes social evaluations of human worth;
  To distinguish economic value from intrinsic human value;
  To investigate how AI reshapes human agency and dignity;
  To explore possible frameworks for valuing human beings beyond capital logic.
current_position: |-
  I currently believe that capital is an indispensable mechanism for organizing economic activity, but it should not become the sole measure of human value.
  As artificial intelligence transforms labor, creativity, judgment, moral responsibility, and human freedom may become increasingly important dimensions of what it means to be human.
