question_description: >-
  Scientific evidence is often treated as the foundation of rational policymaking.
  However, different countries may interpret the same evidence in different ways
  and translate it into very different regulatory choices. This question examines
  why similar scientific evidence can produce divergent technology policies across
  political, institutional, and cultural contexts.

scope: >-
  This research focuses on comparative technology governance, science-policy relations,
  regulatory institutions, risk perception, public trust, and national policy traditions.
  It does not assume that scientific evidence automatically determines policy outcomes.

objectives: >-
  To understand how institutions, political values, public trust, administrative capacity,
  and national development strategies shape the translation of scientific evidence into
  technology policy.

current_position: >-
  My current view is that scientific evidence rarely speaks for itself in policy contexts.
  Evidence must be interpreted, prioritized, institutionalized, and legitimized. Therefore,
  differences in technology policy often reflect not only differences in scientific knowledge,
  but also differences in governance systems, political priorities, and institutional trust.
