question_description: >-
  Artificial intelligence is becoming a general-purpose technology with profound social, economic, and political implications. As AI systems increasingly shape communication, education, labor markets, public services, and decision-making, the question of who should govern AI becomes one of the defining governance challenges of the twenty-first century. This research investigates who possesses the legitimacy, expertise, and authority to establish rules for AI development and deployment.

scope: >-
  This research examines the roles of governments, technology companies, international organizations, academic communities, civil society, and citizens in AI governance. It focuses on governance structures, regulatory institutions, accountability mechanisms, and the balance between innovation and public interest. Technical model development is not the primary focus.

objectives: >-
  To explore competing models of AI governance; to examine how legitimacy, accountability, expertise, and democratic participation can be balanced; and to investigate how governance institutions can adapt to rapidly evolving AI technologies.

current_position: >-
  I currently believe that no single actor can effectively govern artificial intelligence alone. Governments possess legal authority but often lack technical agility. Technology companies possess expertise but may face conflicts of interest. Experts contribute knowledge but may lack democratic legitimacy. Citizens are affected by AI systems but often lack meaningful participation mechanisms. Effective AI governance will likely require a multi-stakeholder model involving governments, industry, experts, and society.
