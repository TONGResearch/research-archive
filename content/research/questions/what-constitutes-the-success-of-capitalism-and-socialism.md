question_description: |-
  Discussions of capitalism and socialism often focus on institutions, yet rarely ask a more fundamental question: by what standards should a social system be judged?
  Should success be measured by GDP, technological innovation, individual liberty, equality, or human flourishing?
  This research examines competing standards for evaluating political and economic systems.
scope: |-
  This research explores the goals, institutional performance, public policy, innovation, welfare, and human development associated with capitalism and socialism.
  Rather than advocating one system, it investigates the criteria by which systems are evaluated.
objectives: |-
  To examine possible standards for evaluating political and economic systems;
  To compare the normative goals of capitalism and socialism;
  To analyze the relationships among efficiency, equality, liberty, and human flourishing;
  To develop a broader framework for institutional evaluation.
current_position: |-
  I currently believe that the success of a social system cannot be measured solely by economic output.
  Sustainable development, human flourishing, institutional resilience, and the capacity to improve people's lives should all be considered when evaluating political and economic systems.
