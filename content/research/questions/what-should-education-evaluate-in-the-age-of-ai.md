question_description: >-
  Generative AI is rapidly changing how knowledge is produced, accessed, and applied.
  If answers become increasingly abundant and inexpensive, traditional educational
  systems may no longer be able to evaluate students using the same standards that
  were designed for a pre-AI world. This question investigates what education should
  evaluate in an age where AI can assist with writing, coding, research, and problem solving.

scope: >-
  This research focuses on higher education, assessment design, AI-native learning,
  critical thinking, human judgment, and human-AI collaboration. It does not attempt
  to evaluate specific AI tools or provide short-term pedagogical recommendations.

objectives: >-
  To examine how generative AI changes the meaning of knowledge, learning, and competence;
  to investigate alternative assessment models; and to explore how educational institutions
  can cultivate human judgment, reasoning, and intellectual responsibility in an AI-enabled world.

current_position: >-
  My current view is that AI is making answers increasingly abundant while making
  judgment increasingly scarce. As a result, educational assessment may gradually shift
  from evaluating final outputs toward evaluating problem formulation, reasoning processes,
  critical evaluation, and the ability to collaborate effectively with AI systems.
