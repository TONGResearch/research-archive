question_description: >-
  An AI-assisted public system must be given objectives and constraints, but an instruction
  such as reducing total cost or expected losses does not settle how benefits and burdens
  should be distributed. A locally important concern may be absent from the objective
  function or represented by an unsuitable proxy. This question asks who should be able
  to identify that mismatch and authorise a revision: developers, domain experts, public
  officials, affected communities, or some combination of them. It also asks how a claim
  to represent the local public interest should itself be scrutinised.
scope: >-
  The initial focus is AI-supported local climate adaptation and public resource allocation.
  Relevant design choices include objective functions, weights, decision thresholds,
  operational constraints, and rules that must not be traded away. Institutional questions
  include legal mandates, expert advice, public participation, procurement, emergency
  powers, and routes of appeal. The inquiry will distinguish correcting a technical error
  from changing the substantive public goals a system is meant to serve.
objectives: >-
  To map who proposes, approves, implements, and reviews changes to system objectives;
  explore procedures for recording reasons and consulting affected groups; and examine
  what happens when participants disagree. A possible later output is a change-control
  framework specifying authority, documentation, testing, and review, rather than leaving
  revisions to informal adjustments by a developer or operator.
current_position: >-
  My provisional position is that public goals should not be set or revised solely through
  technical discretion. Developers and experts should explain feasibility, uncertainty,
  and consequences, while substantive changes require an accountable process consistent
  with the institution's legal mandate and meaningful opportunities for affected people
  to be heard. Neither a public official nor a participating group should automatically
  be treated as the sole voice of the public interest. Authorised changes should remain
  documented, contestable, and subject to review.
