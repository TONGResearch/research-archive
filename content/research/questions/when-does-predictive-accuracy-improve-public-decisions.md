question_description: >-
  A model can be evaluated against observed outcomes, but a public decision must also be
  evaluated against its objectives, available resources, implementation, and consequences.
  This question asks when improvements in predictive accuracy change a public decision
  in a useful way. Local climate adaptation provides a possible setting: would a more
  accurate flood or heat-risk estimate change which neighbourhoods receive assistance,
  when action is taken, or whose losses are prevented? It also asks when uncertainty,
  institutional constraints, or unequal access to services prevent better predictions
  from producing better outcomes.
scope: >-
  The initial focus is machine-learning-supported public decisions, using local climate
  adaptation as a potential case. Relevant issues include calibration, false positives
  and false negatives, changes in conditions between training and use, decision thresholds,
  resource constraints, and the distribution of benefits and harms. Prediction quality,
  decision quality, and implementation quality will be treated as distinct objects of
  evaluation. This question does not assume that predictive performance establishes the
  causal effect of a policy.
objectives: >-
  To develop a framework connecting model outputs to decisions and public outcomes;
  identify when improved predictions would actually change an action; and explore how
  alternative decision rules might be compared under limited resources. Possible work
  includes reviewing policy cases and designing small simulations against a clear
  non-AI baseline, without treating simulated results as evidence of real-world impact.
current_position: >-
  My provisional working hypothesis is that predictive accuracy matters when it is
  relevant to an actionable decision, evaluated under realistic conditions, and connected
  to institutions capable of responding. A better model score alone is not sufficient
  evidence of a better public decision. I would therefore examine uncertainty, error
  costs, implementation capacity, and who benefits alongside predictive performance.
  This position remains open to revision through further reading and case analysis.
