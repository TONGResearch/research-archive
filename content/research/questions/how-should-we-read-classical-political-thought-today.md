question_description: >-
  As artificial intelligence, digital governance,
  and globalization continue to reshape society,
  can the works of Plato, Aristotle, Machiavelli,
  Hobbes, Rousseau, Adam Smith, Marx,
  and other classical thinkers still help us
  understand the world today?

  More importantly,
  should these texts be read as collections of timeless answers,
  or as ways of learning how to ask better questions?

  This research explores the contemporary relevance
  of classical political thought and how it should be
  reinterpreted in the twenty-first century.

scope: >-
  This research focuses on political philosophy,
  political economy,
  the history of political thought,
  and contemporary interpretations of classical texts.

  Particular attention is given to how classical theories
  inform debates on artificial intelligence,
  digital capitalism,
  public policy,
  state capacity,
  and technology governance.

objectives: >-
  To examine the continuing relevance of classical political thought;

  To identify the historical limits and contemporary applicability
  of classical theories;

  To develop a question-driven rather than answer-driven
  approach to reading classical texts;

  And to explore how classical political thought
  can inform contemporary research in governance
  and public policy.

current_position: >-
  I currently believe that the greatest value
  of classical political thought
  does not lie in providing definitive answers
  to contemporary problems.

  Instead,
  its enduring contribution lies in teaching us
  how to observe society,
  understand institutions,
  interpret history,
  and formulate better questions.

  Reading the classics should therefore become
  the beginning of new inquiry,
  rather than the end of discussion.
