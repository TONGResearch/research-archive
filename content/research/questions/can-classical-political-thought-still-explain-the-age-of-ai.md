question_description: >-
  Artificial intelligence is transforming labor,
  governance,
  markets,
  education,
  knowledge production,
  and the relationship between individuals and society.

  In light of these changes,
  can the political thought of Marx,
  Adam Smith,
  Weber,
  Hayek,
  Rawls,
  Foucault,
  and other classical thinkers
  still explain the world we are entering?

  Or has AI created a fundamentally new social reality
  that requires new political theories?

scope: >-
  This research examines the relationship between
  classical political thought
  and the age of artificial intelligence.

  It focuses on labor,
  capital,
  power,
  the state,
  education,
  governance,
  liberty,
  and changing forms of social organization.

  Particular attention is given to identifying
  where classical theories remain applicable
  and where they may require reinterpretation or extension.

objectives: >-
  To evaluate whether classical political thought
  continues to provide explanatory power
  in the age of AI;

  To examine how AI reshapes the historical assumptions
  underlying political theory;

  To connect classical political philosophy
  with contemporary debates on AI governance;

  And to develop theoretical foundations
  for understanding political order
  in the digital age.

current_position: >-
  I currently believe that artificial intelligence
  has not rendered classical political thought obsolete.

  Instead,
  AI has revived many enduring questions
  concerning labor,
  capital,
  power,
  education,
  governance,
  and human freedom.

  While classical theories cannot provide
  ready-made answers to every contemporary challenge,
  they remain indispensable frameworks
  for interpreting technological change.
