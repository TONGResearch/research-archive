question_description: >-
  A local climate adaptation decision support system might produce accurate risk estimates
  yet be difficult for officials to use, overlook vulnerable residents, or offer no clear
  route for challenging a recommendation. Conversely, changes in observed losses may
  reflect weather, infrastructure, or implementation rather than the system itself.
  This question asks how such systems should be evaluated across technical performance,
  practical outcomes, and public accountability. The three dimensions in the title are
  not assumed to be mutually exclusive or reducible to a single score.
scope: >-
  The initial focus is neighbourhood-level flood and heat adaptation. Technical dimensions
  include data quality, calibration, robustness, and uncertainty communication. Operational
  dimensions include usability, cost, staff capacity, timeliness, and whether recommendations
  can be acted upon. Public dimensions include distributional effects, explanations,
  opportunities for contestation, and clear responsibility. Evaluation will distinguish
  model testing, simulated decision performance, and evidence of real-world policy effects.
objectives: >-
  To outline an evaluation framework connecting technical tests, decision processes, and
  public outcomes; identify suitable baselines and observable indicators; and examine how
  disagreements between evaluation dimensions should be reported. Possible later work
  includes an evaluation matrix or a small decision-support prototype. Any claim about
  real-world effectiveness would require an appropriate study rather than model accuracy
  or simulation alone.
current_position: >-
  My provisional position is that these systems need multi-dimensional evaluation.
  Accuracy cannot substitute for usability, equitable treatment, or accountability;
  procedural safeguards alone also do not establish that adaptation has improved.
  Evaluation should make trade-offs visible, identify which requirements are non-negotiable,
  and specify who has authority to judge performance. A system should support better
  public judgement rather than merely produce a more persuasive technical presentation.
