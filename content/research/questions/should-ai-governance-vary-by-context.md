question_description: >-
  Artificial intelligence is increasingly used across education,
  scientific research, software engineering, healthcare, law,
  public administration, and many other domains. Yet the risks,
  objectives, and standards of these fields differ significantly.
  This research investigates whether AI governance should be based
  on universal principles or whether different disciplines and
  contexts require distinct governance frameworks.

scope: >-
  This research examines AI use across multiple domains,
  including education, science, engineering, healthcare,
  law, and government. It focuses on governance principles,
  accountability standards, institutional design, and context-specific
  regulatory approaches rather than technical model development.

objectives: >-
  To evaluate the advantages and limitations of universal AI governance;
  to identify contextual factors that may require differentiated governance
  approaches; and to explore how governance systems can balance consistency,
  flexibility, and legitimacy across diverse domains.

current_position: >-
  I currently believe that no single AI governance framework can be applied
  effectively across all disciplines and contexts. Different domains involve
  different forms of risk, responsibility, expertise, and public interest.
  While some foundational principles may be universal, governance mechanisms
  and evaluation standards should be adapted to the specific context in which
  AI is used.
