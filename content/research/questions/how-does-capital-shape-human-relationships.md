question_description: >-
  Capital is often understood as an economic force,
  yet reading The Communist Manifesto led me to consider
  that its influence extends far beyond the distribution of wealth.
  
  As professions become increasingly commodified,
  family relationships are shaped by economic incentives,
  education responds to market demands,
  and technology becomes a new means of production,
  are human relationships themselves being reorganized by capital?
  
  This research explores how capital shapes social relationships
  and whether capital logic has become one of the dominant principles
  organizing modern society.

scope: >-
  This research focuses on the influence of capital
  on labor relations,
  family structures,
  education,
  social trust,
  professional ethics,
  technological development,
  and digital platform societies.
  
  It combines perspectives from political economy,
  sociology,
  public policy,
  and AI-driven transformations of production.

objectives: >-
  To examine how capital shapes human relationships;
  
  To analyze how capital logic influences modern institutions
  and social values;
  
  To explore the interaction between capital,
  technology,
  and social organization;
  
  And to investigate whether human beings can preserve
  agency and value beyond the logic of capital.

current_position: >-
  I currently believe that capital is not merely an economic resource,
  but also a force that shapes social relationships,
  institutional arrangements,
  and systems of value.
  
  Reading The Communist Manifesto led me to realize
  that Marx's concern extended beyond wealth distribution;
  it was fundamentally about how capital transforms
  the way people relate to one another.
  
  In the age of artificial intelligence,
  capital itself is changing.
  Digital platforms,
  data,
  algorithms,
  and AI may represent new forms of capital,
  making it necessary to revisit this question
  in the context of contemporary society.
